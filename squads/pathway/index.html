How the Leander pathway works

Most swimmers start in our Academy and move up when they're ready, all the way to national finals for some. Others choose Diamond, Masters or a role beyond the pool. This page explains how it all fits together.

Book a squad trialExplore the timelineJoin the Academy waiting list

The short answer

Most swimmers start in our Academy, our learn-to-swim programme for ages 5–10. From there they move into Pre-Squad, then into our competitive squads (training groups organised by ability and age): Sparks, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum and Mercury.

Not everyone wants to race at every level, and that's fine. Diamond is for swimmers who want to keep training without the competition load, and Masters is for adults. Many swimmers also go on to teach, officiate or swim at university.

The competition ladder

Alongside the training pathway there's a competition pathway. Each step needs faster times, and each one is a real achievement.

In 2025/26, Leander swimmers reached every step up to the British / GB pathway: 55 championship qualifications, 29 finals and 25 medals across Counties, Regionals, Nationals, GB Trials and GB Summer Championships. Totals add up results across six championships, so a swimmer may be counted more than once.

Want to see where your swimmer's times sit? Try Race the Standards, and look up their official times and rankings on swimmingresults.org, Swim England's rankings website. Our guide shows you how: swimmingresults explained.

Swim England Talent pathway

Alongside club training and championships, Swim England runs its own Talent pathway: a national route for spotting and developing England's most promising young swimmers. It's a set of development camps and programmes, run first by counties, then regions, then Swim England's national team.

Swimmers are selected. You can't apply. Selection is based on a swimmer's results and their World Aquatics points (formerly FINA points: a score that compares swims across events, ages and genders). Swim England publishes guidance on the times and points it looks at.

Swim England's own pathway diagram shows the steps in order. Each step is a small programme of camps and support, and the ages below are taken from Swim England's 2022–23 selection policy, which is the most recent version we've seen. check with Head Coach or Swim England whether a newer selection policy has replaced the 2022–23 version, and update the ages and numbers below

National Age Group Development ProgrammeCamps for the fastest swimmers in each age group in EnglandSwim England's national talent team13–14

Youth Performance SquadPrepares swimmers for international junior competition, such as the European Junior ChampionshipsSwim England's national talent team18 and under
Senior Performance SquadA small group who could move on to British Swimming's World Class ProgrammeSwim England's national talent teamSenior

After that comes British Swimming's World Class Programme, which is for swimmers from across Great Britain rather than England alone.

How selection works. At the early steps, Swim England looks at the national rankings on swimmingresults.org, mostly for long course (50m pool) swims over a set period. For the county and regional programmes it starts with the 200m individual medley (IM: all four strokes in one race), taking the highest-ranked swimmers first. Where two swimmers are ranked equally, the one with more World Aquatics points is preferred. The national programmes also set a minimum number of points, and the policy says plainly that reaching it doesn't guarantee a place. Coaches can also be consulted, and Swim England can pick swimmers at its discretion when they are clearly improving.

A few other points from the policy are worth knowing. Selected swimmers are usually asked to contribute towards the cost of their camps, there's no individual funding, and there's no right of appeal. Swim England tells selected swimmers directly.

Times and points needed: verify with Head Coach the current time and points guidance for each programme, and which age groups each one is for

Diploma in Sporting Excellence (DiSE). A two-year Swim England programme for talented 16–18 year olds, combining full-time education with high-level swimming support. It's delivered with Loughborough College and runs alongside a swimmer's A levels or BTEC and their club training, leading to a Level 3 qualification. In the 2022–23 policy, swimmers in the right school year who were ranked in the top six in Britain in an Olympic pool event were selected automatically, and further places went to the swimmers with the highest World Aquatics points. confirm current DiSE selection criteria with Swim England or Head Coach

What this means for Leander swimmers. Being selected is a real achievement, but it isn't the only measure of success. Swimmers develop at different ages, and every goal on our pathway is valued. Swim England says itself that talent development is complex and not a straight line. confirm with Head Coach how the club hears about selections and tells families

The best way to follow your swimmer's times and points is swimmingresults explained.

How the season works

The swimming season follows the school year in three terms. Not every race has the same importance. Some sessions make swimmers faster tomorrow; others make them faster three months from now.

Term 1September to DecemberBuild and developWinter Championships
Term 2January to MayBuild and qualifyGB Trials and Regionals
Term 3May to AugustRace and peakSummer Nationals

Two kinds of competition:

  • Priority 1: championships. Regionals, English Nationals, British Championships, British Trials and key qualifying meets. The aim is to perform when it matters.
  • Priority 2: development galas. Club galas and Counties give race experience, a chance to try race plans, and practice at managing nerves on race day.

For younger squads, rankings matter less. The focus is enjoyment, PBs (personal bests) and better technique.

One club, different goals

Every swimmer's goals are different. Here's what they often look like at three ages.

At 9

  • Enjoy competition
  • Learn skills
  • Build confidence

At 14

  • Improve consistency
  • Work towards qualifying times
  • Develop performance

At 17

  • Finals and medals
  • GB international and high-performance swimming, for some
  • University and scholarship ambitions

And for every swimmer who chooses a different route, such as Diamond during exams, coaching in the Academy or coming back as a Master, that's a goal we value just as much.

Our aim is to help every swimmer find out how far they can go.

How moving up is decided

Moving up a squad isn't about age or a single fast swim. Our coaches look at the whole swimmer, using the published Squad standards:

  • Competition: which races they've swum, and for Platinum and Mercury, whether they've reached county or regional qualifying times
  • Training: whether they can complete the sets for the next squad (for example, to move into Bronze a swimmer needs to swim 200m freestyle non-stop)
  • Technique: dives, turns, underwater kicking and stroke skills
  • Routines and attitude: warm-ups, stretching, effort and how they treat their teammates
  • Attendance: each squad has a minimum number of sessions a week. If a swimmer regularly can't meet it, the coaches may suggest a squad that fits better

Every swimmer grows at a different pace, mentally and physically. What matters most is each swimmer's own progress, whatever their level, and being part of the team.

When moves happen: confirm with Head Coach when squad moves are made (e.g. start of term, any time) and how families are told

From the Academy: Pre-Squad swimmers are assessed for a squad place. If another club would suit them better, we'll say so and help.

What this means for your swimmer

  • There's a place for every goal. Learning to swim well, racing at nationals, swimming through exams and coaching others are all valued here.
  • The next step is always clear. Every squad has published standards, so you can see what your swimmer is working towards.
  • It's a long game. Turning up week after week builds confidence, and confident swimmers race better.
  • Ask us. If you're not sure where your swimmer fits, talk to their coach or use Join us.
