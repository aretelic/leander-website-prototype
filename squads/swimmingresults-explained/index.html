swimmingresults.org explained

Where do I find my swimmer's official times, and what do the rankings mean? This guide takes you through it, one step at a time.

Open swimmingresults.org (opens in a new tab)Book a squad trial

The short answer

swimmingresults.org is Swim England's official rankings website. If your swimmer goes to galas, it's the most useful website you'll use. It shows every time they swim at a licensed meet (a gala approved by Swim England at Level 1 to 4, so times are official), their PBs (personal bests), and how they rank against other swimmers of the same age in the county, the region, England and Britain.

It's also where you can check your swimmer's Swim England membership and find entries for national events. It's free and you don't need to log in.

What's on the site

SectionWhat it showsWhy it matters
Individual Best TimesYour swimmer's fastest time in each event, for all time or the last 12 monthsThe quickest way to see their PBs
Personal Swimming RecordEvery time your swimmer has swum at a licensed meetShows progress over time, not just the best swim
Event RankingsThe fastest swimmers in each event, by age group, gender and areaShows where your swimmer sits in the county, region, England and Britain
Membership checkWhether a swimmer's Swim England membership is registeredSwimmers need valid membership to race at licensed meets
National events and entriesEntry pages for Swim England's national championshipsShows who has entered and been accepted

Only times from licensed meets appear. Times from internal club galas and most Academy galas won't be on the site.

Step 1: Find your swimmer

  1. Go to the Individual Best Times page: swimmingresults.org/individualbest.
  2. Choose a period: All Time or Last 12 Months.
  3. Type your swimmer's Swim England membership number or their family name (surname).
  4. If you searched by surname, pick your swimmer from the list. Check the club says Leander.
Screenshot to addIndividual Best Times search page, with the period options and the membership number or family name box highlighted

Where to find the membership number: where Leander families can find their swimmer's Swim England membership number, e.g. TeamUnify or the Swim England membership card. Searching by membership number is quicker and avoids mixing up swimmers with the same surname.

Tip: save the results page as a bookmark on your phone, so it's one tap away at galas.

Step 2: Read their PBs

The results page lists your swimmer's best time in each event they've swum at a licensed meet.

Screenshot to adda swimmer's Individual Best Times page, with the short course and long course tables labelled and personal details blurred
  • Each row is one event, for example 100m freestyle or 200m individual medley (IM: all four strokes in one race).
  • The time is their PB for that event in that pool length.
  • Short course and long course are listed separately (see Step 4).
  • confirm the columns shown on the results page, e.g. date, meet, licence level, World Aquatics points, and explain each

Want every swim, not just the best? Use the Personal Swimming Record page, which lists all of your swimmer's times at licensed meets. It's a good way to see steady progress, even when a PB hasn't come yet.

Screenshot to addPersonal Swimming Record page for one event, showing several swims over a season, personal details blurred

Times look wrong or missing? Results usually appear a little while after a meet, once they've been checked and uploaded. typical time for results to appear after a meet, and who families contact at Leander if a time is missing or wrong

Step 3: Understand rankings

Rankings list the fastest swimmers in an event over a period, so you can see where your swimmer sits.

  1. Go to Event Rankings (the last 12 months is the most useful view).
  2. Choose the event (stroke and distance) and the pool length (short course or long course).
  3. Choose gender and age group.
  4. Choose the area: county, region, England or Britain.
Screenshot to addEvent Rankings search page with event, pool length, gender, age and area options labelled

Why rankings matter more as swimmers get older

For younger swimmers, rankings are mostly a bit of fun. They start to matter more the further a swimmer goes. Swim England uses the national rankings on this site to pick swimmers for its Talent pathway camps, starting with county camps at around age 11. In its 2022–23 selection policy, it mostly used long course (50m pool) times swum over a set period, and where two swimmers were ranked equally it preferred the one with more World Aquatics points. University and college coaches also look at a swimmer's times and points when they're recruiting. You can read more in How the Leander pathway works and University and scholarships.

confirm whether swimmingresults.org shows World Aquatics points next to each time, so families know where to find them

Keep rankings in perspective. Rankings change every week as other swimmers race, and younger swimmers develop at very different rates. For younger squads, the focus is enjoyment, PBs and better technique, not rankings. A bad race is an event, not an identity.

Step 4: Short course (SC) and long course (LC)

  • Short course (SC) means a 25m pool.
  • Long course (LC) means a 50m pool, the size used at the Olympics.

Times are recorded and ranked separately for each, because the same race is usually faster in a 25m pool: there are more turns, and a swimmer gains speed pushing off the wall.

So don't compare a short course time with a long course one directly. Some championships accept both, and the site has an equivalent time converter that gives a rough conversion. confirm whether Surrey and London Region qualifying accepts both SC and LC times, and whether conversions are used

Screenshot to addthe same swimmer's 100m freestyle shown in the short course and long course tables, side by side, personal details blurred

Step 5: Check membership

Swimmers need a valid Swim England membership to race at licensed meets. You can check your swimmer's membership is registered using Swim England's membership check, linked from the Members menu on swimmingresults.org: swimming.org/swimengland/membership-check.

Screenshot to addSwim England membership check page, with the search box highlighted

Step 6: National event entries

When Swim England runs a national championship, swimmingresults.org has a page for it under National events and entries. You'll find entry information and the lists of swimmers entered and accepted.

Screenshot to addNational events and entries menu, with one championship's entry page open

how national championship entries are made for Leander swimmers (e.g. coach-led or by families) and who to contact

What this means for your swimmer

  • Bookmark it. It's the official record of your swimmer's racing.
  • Celebrate progress, not just PBs. The Personal Swimming Record shows how far they've come.
  • Check the details. Coaches use these times for gala and championship entries, so let us know if something looks wrong.
  • Use it for the next step. University and college coaches often ask for a link to a swimmer's times. See University and scholarships.
