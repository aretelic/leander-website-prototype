University swimming and scholarships

Can swimming help my child at university? Often, yes. Here's how it works in the UK and the US, explained.

Book a squad trialSquad standards

The short answer

Our squad swimmers are regularly offered places at top swimming universities. In 2026, nine Mercury swimmers left for university.

Swimming can open doors in two main ways:

  • UK universities with performance swimming programmes, which offer high-level training alongside a degree
  • US colleges, some of which offer sports scholarships that can cover part of the cost of studying

There's no single place to look this up. Families find out by researching each university's swimming programme themselves and contacting the coaches directly. This page shows you how, and how Leander can help.

Recruiters often compare swimmers using World Aquatics points. This page explains those too.

The general information below is a guide only. Rules and requirements change, so always check with the university, college or governing body directly.

Research it yourself: there's no central list

There's no central website or list of every university swimming programme, its entry times and what support it offers. Each university runs its own programme, sets its own standards and changes them from year to year.

In practice, families who've been through it say the way to find out is to:

  1. Make a shortlist. Start with universities that offer the course your swimmer wants to study, then check which of them have a performance swimming programme (a squad with professional coaches and regular pool time).
  2. Read each programme's own web pages. Look for entry times or standards, how many sessions a week, the pool (25m or 50m), and any sports scholarship or bursary.
  3. Contact the programme's coach directly. This is often the only way to find out what they're really looking for this year.
  4. Compare academic and swimming requirements together. A swimming place doesn't replace an academic offer.

How to approach a university or college coach

University and college coaches expect to hear from swimmers and families. A short, clear email is the best start.

What to include

  • Your swimmer's name, age, school year and the course they hope to study
  • Their main events and PBs (personal bests), for both short course (25m pool) and long course (50m pool)
  • A link to their times on swimmingresults.org, Swim England's official rankings website (see swimmingresults explained)
  • Their World Aquatics points for their best events, if you have them
  • Their current club (Leander) and coach, and their predicted or achieved grades
  • A question or two: what times the programme looks for, how training fits around the course, and whether there's a scholarship or bursary

Tips

  • The swimmer should write the email where possible. Coaches want to hear from the athlete.
  • Talk to your Leander coach first. They can help you judge which programmes are realistic and may be able to speak to the university coach.
  • Ask about visits. Many programmes welcome a visit to watch or join a session.
  • Keep a simple record of who you've contacted, when, and what they said.

UK university performance programmes

What they are

Several UK universities run performance swimming programmes: a squad with professional coaches, regular pool time, and often strength and conditioning, physiotherapy and help fitting training around study. Some are linked to British Swimming's performance centres.

What swimmers usually get

Support varies a lot between universities, but can include:

  • Daily coached training, often in a 50m pool
  • Strength and conditioning, physiotherapy and sports science
  • Flexibility with timetables and deadlines around major competitions
  • A sports scholarship or bursary at some universities, which may include money, kit or gym membership

How you get in

  • You still need an academic offer. You apply through UCAS like any other student. Most universities don't lower their grade requirements for athletes, though a few have schemes that may take sporting achievement into account.
  • Talk to the swimming programme separately. Programmes usually have their own entry times or standards and their own application or trial process. See How to approach a university or college coach.
  • Start early. Many swimmers start talking to university coaches in Year 12, before applying through UCAS in Year 13.

Competing at university

University swimmers race for their university at BUCS (British Universities and Colleges Sport) championships, and many keep racing for a home club in the holidays and at national championships.

To give you a sense of scale, Stirling publishes its entry standards: at least 750 World Aquatics points for its High Performance squad and at least 675 for its Performance Development squad, and you also need a place on a Stirling course. Other universities set their own standards, so always check.

The UK's leading university programmes, explained

University swimming in the UK works at roughly three levels, and most big universities offer more than one of them.

  1. Performance centres. Aquatics GB (the new name for British Swimming) runs its World Class Programme from a small number of performance centres. Two are at universities, Bath and Loughborough, and the third is in Manchester. These train Olympic and international swimmers, and places are by selection.
  2. University high-performance programmes. Several universities run their own elite squads with full-time coaches, strength and conditioning, and sports science. Some work closely with a strong local club. Entry is usually by times or points.
  3. BUCS competitive squads and recreational swimming. Most universities have a student swimming club with coached sessions and a team that races at BUCS (British Universities and Colleges Sport) championships and in local student leagues. Many also have a development or recreational squad for people who want to keep fit and stay part of a team.

How to approach them

Performance centres and high-performance programmes usually want to see a swimmer's long course and short course times, their World Aquatics points and their recent progress. Most publish selection criteria or a contact for new applicants, and some ask you to fill in a form. Student clubs at other universities are usually happy to hear from you directly, and many hold trials in freshers' week. In every case, the course comes first: you need an academic offer as well. The section on how to approach a coach above has a checklist.

US college swimming and NCAA scholarships

How it works

Most US college sport is run by the NCAA (National Collegiate Athletic Association). College swimming teams train and compete at a high level, and many British swimmers have swum in the US.

DivisionIn brief
Division IThe most competitive level. Can offer athletic scholarships
Division IICompetitive. Can offer athletic scholarships
Division IIICan't offer athletic scholarships, though students may get academic or need-based financial help

There are also colleges outside the NCAA (for example in the NAIA and junior college system) that offer swimming scholarships.

What a scholarship covers

Swimming scholarships are often partial: a coach may split their budget across several swimmers, so a scholarship might cover some of the tuition, accommodation or other costs rather than all of them. Rules on scholarships and team sizes in Division I changed significantly in 2025, so check the latest NCAA guidance. In brief, many Division I colleges no longer have a fixed number of swimming scholarships, but they now have a limit on how many swimmers and divers they can have on the team (reported as 30 per team, and lower in some conferences), so places on some teams are harder to get.

What you'll need

  • Academic eligibility. NCAA Division I and II swimmers must register with the NCAA Eligibility Center, which checks academic qualifications such as GCSEs and A levels. Individual colleges set their own admissions requirements, which may include tests such as the SAT or ACT.
  • Amateur status. There are rules about prize money and payments in sport. Check them before accepting any money.
  • Times. College coaches recruit on times, often compared using World Aquatics points.
  • Contact rules. There are rules on when college coaches can contact swimmers. Check the current NCAA calendar.

When to start

Many swimmers begin researching around age 15–16 and are in contact with college coaches during sixth form. Some families use a recruitment agency. If you do, check exactly what you're paying for.

World Aquatics points (formerly FINA points)

What they are

World Aquatics points are a score that compares swims across different events, ages and genders. FINA, swimming's world governing body, changed its name to World Aquatics in 2022, so you'll still see both names.

How they work

Each event has a base time, set from the world record and updated regularly. Swim the base time and you score 1,000 points. The slower the swim, the lower the score, and the scale isn't a straight line: each second matters more the closer you get to the top.

Points are calculated separately for short course (25m pool) and long course (50m pool).

Why recruiters use them

A 100m butterflyer and a 1500m freestyler are hard to compare by time alone. Points let university and college coaches compare swimmers across events, and see quickly roughly where a swimmer would fit in their team.

How to find your swimmer's points

Start with swimmingresults.org, Swim England's official rankings website. It shows every time your swimmer has swum at a licensed meet (a competition approved by Swim England, so times are official). Diploma in Sporting Excellence (DiSE)

The Diploma in Sporting Excellence (DiSE) is a two-year Swim England programme for talented swimmers aged 16–18. It combines full-time education with high-level support from Swim England, and is one of the later steps on the Swim England Talent pathway (Swim England's route for spotting and developing England's most promising young swimmers).

DiSE is delivered with Loughborough College and runs over two school years alongside a swimmer's A levels or BTEC and their normal club training. In the 2022–23 selection policy, swimmers ranked in the top six in Britain in an Olympic pool event were picked automatically, and the remaining places went to those with the highest World Aquatics points.

Useful resources for parents

A Leander parent who has been through all this recommends these. They're the places families end up relying on, and they matter more the further a swimmer goes.

  • swimmingresults.org, Swim England's official rankings and membership website. It has your swimmer's official times, PBs and rankings, and it's where university coaches will look. Our guide shows you how to use it: swimmingresults explained.
  • World Aquatics points, which compare swims across events, ages and genders. Universities, US colleges and Swim England's Talent pathway all use them. See World Aquatics points above.
  • Swim England's resources for swimmers and parents. Guides from Swim England's talent team on nutrition and hydration, race day, managing nerves, health (including periods and concussion), technique, open water and para-swimming, plus the Talent pathway selection policies.
  • Swim England's performance pathway diagram. A one-page picture of every step from county development camps to British Swimming's World Class Programme, with DiSE alongside the national youth programmes.
  • Swim England's Talent pathway selection policies. How swimmers are picked for each step, including the ages and the minimum World Aquatics points. What this means for your swimmer

  • University swimming is one goal among many. Swimming at university for fun, coaching, or stepping back from competition are all routes we value.
  • Grades matter as much as times. Academic offers and eligibility come first.
  • Expect to do the research. There's no central list, so set aside time to look at each programme and contact coaches directly.
  • Start the conversation early. If your swimmer is 15 or over and interested, talk to their coach.
