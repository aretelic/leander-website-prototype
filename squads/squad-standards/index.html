Squad standards: what each squad expects

What does a swimmer need to show before moving up a squad? Here's the full answer, in plain English.

Book a squad trialRace the Standards

The short answer

Each squad has published standards: what a swimmer needs to show before moving up to it. They cover typical age, racing experience, sessions a week, technique and habits. Coaches use them as a guide, not a checklist, because every swimmer grows at a different pace, mentally and physically.

What matters most is each swimmer's own progress, whatever their level, and being part of the team.

Some terms we use

  • Squad: a competitive training group, grouped by ability and age.
  • Gala: a swimming competition.
  • Official races: the 18 individual events swum at licensed competitions: 50m, 100m, 200m, 400m, 800m and 1500m freestyle; 50m, 100m and 200m backstroke, breaststroke and butterfly; and 100m (short course only), 200m and 400m individual medley (IM: all four strokes in one race). Head Coach to confirm this is the list of 18 events the standards refer to
  • County qualifier: a swimmer who has swum a county qualifying time, the time needed to enter the Surrey County Age Group Championships.
  • Regional qualifier: the same, for the London Regional Championships.
  • "3 × 400 on 7'30": swim 400m three times, starting a new one every 7 minutes 30 seconds. The faster you swim, the more rest you get.
  • Underwaters: the fast underwater kicking after a dive or turn. "7.5m" means kicking underwater to the 7.5m mark before surfacing. "The flags" are the backstroke flags, 5m from each end.
  • Pull-outs: the special underwater stroke breaststrokers use after a dive or turn.
  • Turnover: the short gap between one set and the next.
  • Therabands: stretchy resistance bands used for shoulder exercises.

What this means, squad by squad

Sparks

Our youngest squad, and the first step from the Academy into squad swimming.

  • Typical age: Sparks typical age
  • Entry standard: Sparks entry standard, e.g. completed Pre-Squad
  • Sessions: Sparks sessions per week (old website said 2 minimum, 2 recommended)
  • Technique and routines: Sparks technique focus and routines

In plain English: one or two sentences from the Head Coach on what Sparks is for

Bronze

Our biggest competitive squad, where good habits start.

In plain English: your swimmer doesn't need to have raced yet, though a first gala helps. They can swim 200m freestyle without stopping and are learning the basics that make everything else easier: a good body position in the water, streamlining (a long, tight shape after dives and turns), breathing and dives. They'll learn to warm up and look after their shoulders. Two sessions a week is the minimum; four gets the most out of it.

Silver

Racing most events and building stamina.

In plain English: your swimmer has raced in most official events and can swim 400m freestyle without stopping. Coaches are looking for good dives and technique that holds up over a 600m swim. Three sessions a week minimum, four recommended.

Gold

Racing almost everything, and training like it.

In plain English: your swimmer has raced every official event except the longest and hardest ones (200m butterfly, 400m IM and the 800m/1500m freestyle). They can swim repeat 400s on a set time and hold good technique over 1000m. Turns and underwater kicking start to matter a lot. Four sessions a week minimum, five recommended.

Platinum

County swimmers starting to specialise.

In plain English: your swimmer has a county qualifying time and has raced 16 of the 18 official events. They start to focus on their best strokes and distances, and to take more responsibility, such as using therabands for their own shoulder exercises. Four sessions a week minimum, five recommended.

Mercury

Regional and national swimmers, and role models for the whole club.

In plain English: your swimmer has a regional qualifying time in at least two events. Training is about race strategy and fine detail, and Mercury swimmers lead warm-ups and act as role models for younger squads. Six sessions a week minimum. At this level training becomes more individual, and recovery, sleep and nutrition matter more.

Diamond

Keep swimming, your way, and stay part of the team.

Diamond is a non-competitive squad and one of our biggest (38 swimmers in October 2026). It's for swimmers who want to keep training without the competition load, including those easing off for GCSEs or A levels.

  • Typical age: Diamond typical age range
  • Entry standard: Diamond entry standard, and whether it's open to new joiners
  • Sessions: Diamond sessions per week (old website said 1 minimum, 3 recommended)
  • Technique and routines: Diamond technique focus and routines

In plain English: Diamond is a positive choice that we value as much as racing. More on Diamond.

What this means for your swimmer

  • The standards show the next step. They aren't a deadline. Swimmers move up when they're ready.
  • Attendance matters. Repeated gaps matter more than one missed session. If a swimmer regularly can't make the minimum, the coaches may suggest a squad that fits better.
  • Times aren't everything. Technique, routines and attitude count as much as speed.
  • Not sure where your swimmer sits? Ask their coach, or compare a PB with county times using Race the Standards.
