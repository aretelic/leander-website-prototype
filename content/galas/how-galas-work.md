# How galas work

## What is a gala, and what happens at one?

A **gala** (also called a **meet**) is a swimming competition. This page explains the different kinds, how your swimmer gets entered, what happens on the day and how you can help.

[PHOTO: swimmers on the blocks at the start of a race, from behind, Leander caps]

---

## The short answer

Galas range from friendly club races for our youngest swimmers to national championships. Your swimmer's coach will suggest which galas suit them, and you enter through TeamUnify, our members' system. On the day, the coaches look after the swimming. Your job is to support.

*Let the coach coach. Let the swimmer swim. Let the parent support.*

## The types of gala

Swimmers usually work up from club galas to open meets and then championships. Not every gala matters equally: some are about racing experience, some are about qualifying, and a few are about performing when it matters most.

| Type | What it is | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal club galas** | Races just for Leander swimmers, such as our gala between squads | Squad swimmers getting race practice in a friendly setting |
| **Academy galas** | Our own galas for Academy swimmers. Often a swimmer's very first race | Academy swimmers (ages 5–10) {{TBC: which Academy stages can enter}} |
| **Annual Squad Relay** | A fun evening of relay racing that mixes all ages, so our youngest swimmers race alongside our seniors | All squads |
| **Licensed open meets (Level 4 to Level 1)** | Galas run under a Swim England licence, open to swimmers from many clubs. Times count for rankings and for qualifying for championships | Squad swimmers, starting with Level 4 |
| **Championships** | County, regional and national championships. You need a qualifying time to enter | Swimmers who've swum the qualifying time |

### Licensed meets: what Level 4, 3, 2 and 1 mean

Swim England gives each open meet a licence level.

- **Level 4** is for newer racers. A good first open meet.
- **Level 3** is the next step up. Many Level 3 meets use **upper-limit times**: if a swimmer is already faster than the limit, they can't enter that event. This keeps the racing fair for developing swimmers.
- **Level 2** and **Level 1** are more competitive. Level 1 is national level.

Because these meets are licensed, the times swimmers achieve count officially. They go into the national rankings, and they can be used as qualifying times for championships.

### Championships: the competition ladder

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| Local galas | Club and open meets. Enjoy competing, learn skills, build confidence |
| Counties | The county championships. You need a **county qualifying time** |
| London Regionals | The regional championships. You need a **regional qualifying time** |
| English Nationals | National championships for England |
| British and GB pathway | British Championships and GB Trials |
| International | Representing your country |

**Qualifying time:** the time you need to swim to enter a championship.
**Consideration time:** a slightly slower time that may still earn a place if there's space.
**PB:** personal best, the fastest time a swimmer has done in that event.
**SC / LC:** short course (a 25m pool) and long course (a 50m pool). Times are recorded separately for each.

See how your swimmer's times compare with county times: [Race the Standards].

### How the season works

| Term | Focus | Leads to |
|---|---|---|
| Term 1 (September to December) | Build and develop | Winter championships |
| Term 2 (January to May) | Build and qualify | GB Trials and Regionals |
| Term 3 (May to August) | Race and peak | Summer Nationals |

The galas that matter most are the championships: Regionals, English Nationals, British Championships and GB Trials. Club galas and Counties are **development galas**: they give race experience, a chance to try race plans, and practice at handling nerves on race day.

## What to expect on the day

**Before the races**
- **Arrive in good time for the warm-up.** Coaches plan the warm-up around the time available. Most galas and regionals have short warm-ups and little space on poolside, so being late really matters.
- **Find the team.** Swimmers sit with the Leander team and coaches, not with their parents. {{TBC: confirm where swimmers sit and how they meet the team}}
- **Check the programme.** It lists every event and heat. A **heat** is one race within an event; big events have many heats.

**Around each race**
- **Briefing before, debrief after.** Swimmers should ask the coach for guidance before their race, and go back to the coach afterwards to talk it through.
- **Recover straight away.** Whenever possible, swimmers do their recovery swim or routine straight after the race, before going back to the stands.

**Keeping it in perspective**
- **For younger swimmers, the focus is on enjoyment, PBs and technique,** not rankings or medals.
- **Independence grows with age.** We help swimmers get the balance right between doing things for themselves and taking the coach's guidance.
- *A bad race is an event, not an identity.*

## swimmingresults.org: your most useful website

<!-- source: https://www.swimmingresults.org/ -->
If your swimmer goes to galas, **swimmingresults.org** is the website to bookmark. It's run by Swim England, and it's the official record of every time your swimmer swims at a **licensed meet** (a gala approved by Swim England at Level 1 to 4).

**What you can see there**
- **Your swimmer's times and PBs.** Search for your swimmer by surname or Swim England membership number on the **Individual Best Times** page: [swimmingresults.org/individualbest](https://www.swimmingresults.org/individualbest/). Times are shown separately for **short course** (25m pool) and **long course** (50m pool).
- **Rankings.** How your swimmer's times compare with other swimmers of the same age, in each event, in the **county**, the **region**, **nationally** and across **Britain**.
- **National event entries.** Entry pages and accepted entries for Swim England's national championships.
- **Membership check.** Whether your swimmer's Swim England membership is registered and up to date. Swimmers need it to race at licensed meets.

**Good to know**
- Only times from **licensed meets** appear. Times from internal club galas and most Academy galas won't be there.
- Times usually appear a little while after the meet, once the results have been checked and uploaded. {{TBC: typical time for results to appear on swimmingresults.org after a meet}}
- Coaches use these times when entering swimmers for galas and championships, so it's worth checking they're right.

## How parents can help

**Please help with**
- Getting to galas on time
- Sleep, food and recovery routines
- Organisation: entries, travel and kit
- Encouragement and perspective

**Please avoid**
- Coaching from the poolside or the stands
- Comparing swimmers
- Focusing only on times and medals
- Pressure after races

**On the tough days:** listen before trying to fix things, keep perspective and trust the long-term process.

**Photos and filming:** we follow Swim England guidance on photography at competitions. Many venues have their own rules, so check before you film. See our [Photography policy].

## Why officials matter

Every gala needs **officials**: trained volunteers such as timekeepers, judges and referees. Without enough officials, a meet can't run under a licence, and swimmers' times don't count.

That's why we always need more. Volunteering is also a rewarding way to be part of race day.

**How to become an official**
1. Take the online Judge 1 course (from age 15).
2. Train on poolside as a mentored trainee.
3. Qualify as a Judge 1, usually within a year.

Squad swimmers can also get experience by helping at our own Academy galas. See [Beyond the pool].

## What this means for your swimmer

- **Start small.** First galas are about learning what racing feels like. A ribbon, a PB or simply finishing is a great result.
- **Follow the coach's advice** on which galas to enter.
- **Expect to move up the ladder over years, not weeks.** Club galas lead to open meets, then to championships.
- **Every gala is practice for the next one.** Even a hard day teaches something.
