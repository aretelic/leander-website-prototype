## What is a Level 3 meet?

Swim England licenses open meets at four levels. **Level 4** is for newer racers, and **Level 1** is national level. **Level 3** sits in between: it's the next step for swimmers who've raced at club galas and Level 4 meets.

Because the meet is licensed, every time counts. Times go into the national rankings and can be used as **qualifying times** (the times needed to enter county, regional or national championships).

**Upper-limit times** are the fastest a swimmer can be to enter an event. They're not entry standards to reach: they're a ceiling, so the meet stays focused on developing swimmers.

## What to expect (especially for first-timers)

- **Warm-up before each session.** Warm-ups are short and poolside space is tight, so arrive on time. {{TBC: warm-up times and lane allocations}}
- **Heats, no finals.** {{TBC: confirm format}} Each race is a **heat**. Results are decided on time across all the heats in an age group, so you can win a medal from any heat.
- **Seeded slowest to fastest.** {{TBC: confirm}} Swimmers race others of a similar speed.
- **Over-the-top starts.** {{TBC: confirm}} The next heat starts while the previous swimmers are still in the water, which keeps the meet moving.
- **Swimmers sit with their club team and coaches.** Parents watch from the spectator area.
- **No penalty for swimming fast.** {{TBC: confirm}} If a swimmer beats the upper-limit time on the day, the swim still counts.

## Volunteers and officials needed

A licensed meet can't run without qualified **officials**: trained volunteers such as timekeepers, judges and referees. If there aren't enough, times don't count.

- **Visiting clubs:** please offer licensed officials for one or more sessions. {{TBC: officials' organiser contact}}
