# Athlete support

**What does a Leander swimmer get beyond pool sessions?** Strength and conditioning, camps, clinics with Olympians, education, and a clear route when something goes wrong.

## The short answer

Swimming fast takes more than swimming. Our squad swimmers train on land with **Move4Sport**, our strength and conditioning partner, go on **training camps**, learn from **Olympians** and specialists at clinics, and get practical guidance on school, exams, nutrition and being a female athlete. Coaches, swimmers and parents each have a clear role.

## Strength and conditioning with Move4Sport

**S&C (strength and conditioning)** is training on land to support swimming: building strength, power and movement that hold up in the water.

### Who they are
- **Move4Sport** is a team of strength and conditioning specialists with 40+ years of combined experience, based in West Wickham.
- Their Head Coach, **Gareth Shelbourne**, is **UKSCA S&C Coach of the Year 2026** (UKSCA is the UK Strength and Conditioning Association).
- **Dan Kennett** and **Matt Rees** are the main coaches for Leander swimmers.
- All are qualified, accredited and DBS checked, with safeguarding and first aid training.

### Why it matters for swimmers
1. **Faster starts and turns.** Strong, explosive legs and a stable trunk give more power off the blocks and the wall.
2. **Resilient shoulders and backs.** Strength work helps bodies cope with thousands of strokes every week.
3. **Growing safely.** Training adapts through growth spurts, so swimmers keep progressing.
4. **Athletes for life.** Strong, well-moving swimmers who understand their bodies, in and out of the pool.

### How S&C develops with age
The right training at the right time, based on each swimmer's stage of development, not just their age. This is called **long-term athlete development**.

| | Learn to Train | Train to Train | Train to Compete |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ages** | 9 to 12 | 12 to 16 | 16+ |
| **Focus** | Movement skills, coordination and confidence | Building a strength base safely through growth | Swimming-specific strength, power and resilience |
| **Equipment** | Bodyweight, bands and medicine balls | Gradual progression to free weights | Barbell lifts, heavier loads, power and speed |
| **They learn** | How to move well and warm up properly | Why we train and how growth affects them | Recovery, nutrition and sleep, and owning their training |

### A typical squad S&C session
Warm-up → prep and prehab (exercises that help prevent injury) → speed and power → main strength set → work capacity (building the fitness to keep going)

Sessions are planned with Leander's coaches around pool training and the gala calendar, so swimmers are supported, never overloaded. S&C sessions count towards a squad's sessions per week.

### Being a female athlete
Training changes as swimmers grow. Our education sessions help swimmers and parents understand **nutrition** (fuelling training and recovery) and **training and competing around the menstrual cycle**, so nobody has to work it out alone. {{TBC: whether these sessions are open beyond the clinic, and for which squads}}

### Managing school and training
Swimming and school can work together. Our guidance:
- **Consistency matters.** Repeated gaps matter more than one missed session. Skills, fitness, underwaters and turns all need repetition.
- **Ownership grows with age.** At 8 to 12, swimmers listen, learn routines and build good habits. At 13 to 18, they own their goals, attendance and preparation.
- **At the top level, recovery counts.** Training becomes more individual, and sleep and nutrition matter more.

### Swimming through exams
- **Keep a routine and adjust the load, rather than stopping.** Fewer sessions can be better than none.
- **Swimming is a healthy break from study.**
- **Tell the coach early** so we can plan around exams.
- **Diamond is an option.** Some swimmers move to Diamond, our non-competitive squad, to keep swimming without the competition load. {{TBC: how swimmers move between Diamond and the competitive squads around exams}}

## Working together

**Let the coach coach. Let the swimmer swim. Let the parent support.**

| Role | Their job |
|---|---|
| **Coaches** | Create the environment |
| **Swimmers** | Embrace the opportunity |
| **Parents** | Support the swimmer |

### How parents can help most
| Please help with | Please avoid |
|---|---|
| Attendance and punctuality | Coaching from the poolside |
| Sleep, food and recovery routines | Comparing swimmers |
| Organisation and getting to competitions | Focusing only on times and medals |
| Encouragement and perspective | Pressure after races |

### On the tough days
Listen before solving, keep perspective and trust the long-term process. **A bad race is an event, not an identity.**

As swimmers get older, the relationship changes: *Parent → Coach → Athlete* becomes *Athlete ↔ Coach*. Older swimmers talk to their coach directly about goals, training and races.
