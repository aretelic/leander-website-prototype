# swimmingresults.org explained

**Where do I find my swimmer's official times, and what do the rankings mean?** This guide takes you through it, one step at a time.

[PHOTO: parent on poolside looking at a phone, screen not visible, swimmers racing in the background]

---

## The short answer

**swimmingresults.org** is Swim England's official rankings website. If your swimmer goes to galas, it's the most useful website you'll use. It shows every time they swim at a **licensed meet** (a gala approved by Swim England at Level 1 to 4, so times are official), their **PBs** (personal bests), and how they **rank** against other swimmers of the same age in the county, the region, England and Britain.

It's also where you can **check your swimmer's Swim England membership** and find **entries for national events**. It's free and you don't need to log in.

---

## What's on the site

| Section | What it shows | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual Best Times** | Your swimmer's fastest time in each event, for all time or the last 12 months | The quickest way to see their PBs |
| **Personal Swimming Record** | Every time your swimmer has swum at a licensed meet | Shows progress over time, not just the best swim |
| **Event Rankings** | The fastest swimmers in each event, by age group, gender and area | Shows where your swimmer sits in the county, region, England and Britain |
| **Membership check** | Whether a swimmer's Swim England membership is registered | Swimmers need valid membership to race at licensed meets |
| **National events and entries** | Entry pages for Swim England's national championships | Shows who has entered and been accepted |

**Only times from licensed meets appear.** Times from internal club galas and most Academy galas won't be on the site.

## Step 1: Find your swimmer

1. Go to the **Individual Best Times** page: [swimmingresults.org/individualbest](https://www.swimmingresults.org/individualbest/).
2. Choose a **period**: **All Time** or **Last 12 Months**.
3. Type your swimmer's **Swim England membership number** or their **family name** (surname).
4. If you searched by surname, pick your swimmer from the list. Check the club says Leander.

## Step 2: Read their PBs

The results page lists your swimmer's best time in each event they've swum at a licensed meet.

[SCREENSHOT: a swimmer's Individual Best Times page, with the short course and long course tables labelled and personal details blurred]

- **Each row is one event**, for example 100m freestyle or 200m individual medley (IM: all four strokes in one race).
- **The time** is their PB for that event in that pool length.
- **Short course and long course are listed separately** (see Step 4).

## Step 3: Understand rankings

Rankings list the fastest swimmers in an event over a period, so you can see where your swimmer sits.

1. Go to **Event Rankings** (the last 12 months is the most useful view).
2. Choose the **event** (stroke and distance) and the **pool length** (short course or long course).
3. Choose **gender** and **age group**.
4. Choose the **area**: county, region, England or Britain.

[SCREENSHOT: Event Rankings search page with event, pool length, gender, age and area options labelled]

**What the levels mean for Leander swimmers**

| Ranking | Who it compares your swimmer with |
|---|---|
| **County** | Swimmers in Surrey |
| **Regional** | Swimmers in the Swim England London Region |
| **National** | Swimmers across England |
| **British** | Swimmers across Great Britain |

### Why rankings matter more as swimmers get older

<!-- source: Swim England, "Talent Pathway Selection Policies and Guidelines 2022-2023", https://swimming.app.box.com/s/a1jbs0mi6kus1nq8mtow77u25nusbnmw -->
<!-- source: https://www.swimming.org/sport/swimmers-parents/ -->
For younger swimmers, rankings are mostly a bit of fun. They start to matter more the further a swimmer goes. Swim England uses the national rankings on this site to pick swimmers for its Talent pathway camps, starting with county camps at around age 11. In its 2022–23 selection policy, it mostly used long course (50m pool) times swum over a set period, and where two swimmers were ranked equally it preferred the one with more World Aquatics points. University and college coaches also look at a swimmer's times and points when they're recruiting. You can read more in [LINK: How the Leander pathway works] and [LINK: University and scholarships].

{{TBC: confirm whether swimmingresults.org shows World Aquatics points next to each time, so families know where to find them}}

**Keep rankings in perspective.** Rankings change every week as other swimmers race, and younger swimmers develop at very different rates. For younger squads, the focus is enjoyment, PBs and better technique, not rankings. *A bad race is an event, not an identity.*

## Step 4: Short course (SC) and long course (LC)

- **Short course (SC)** means a **25m pool**.
- **Long course (LC)** means a **50m pool**, the size used at the Olympics.

Times are recorded and ranked **separately** for each, because the same race is usually faster in a 25m pool: there are more turns, and a swimmer gains speed pushing off the wall.

So don't compare a short course time with a long course one directly. Some championships accept both, and the site has an **equivalent time converter** that gives a rough conversion. {{TBC: confirm whether Surrey and London Region qualifying accepts both SC and LC times, and whether conversions are used}}

## Step 5: Check membership

Swimmers need a valid **Swim England membership** to race at licensed meets. You can check your swimmer's membership is registered using Swim England's **membership check**, linked from the **Members** menu on swimmingresults.org: [swimming.org/swimengland/membership-check](https://www.swimming.org/swimengland/membership-check/).

## What this means for your swimmer

- **Bookmark it.** It's the official record of your swimmer's racing.
- **Celebrate progress, not just PBs.** The Personal Swimming Record shows how far they've come.
- **Check the details.** Coaches use these times for gala and championship entries, so let us know if something looks wrong.
- **Use it for the next step.** University and college coaches often ask for a link to a swimmer's times. See [LINK: University and scholarships].
