What does my swimmer need?

Join the Academy waiting listVisit the club shop

The short answer

Every swimmer starts with three things: a costume, a swimming hat and pool goggles. As swimmers move up through the squads, they add training equipment, one piece at a time. You don't need to buy anything until your swimmer's stage or squad uses it, so there's no need to buy ahead.

The squads, squad by squad

A squad is a competitive training group, grouped by ability and age. Squad swimmers build up their training equipment in roughly this order:

  1. Kickboard: a float held in front of the body, so the swimmer can practise kicking on its own.
  2. Pull buoy: a float held between the thighs. It stops the legs kicking, so the swimmer works on their arms and upper body.
  3. Snorkel: a swimming snorkel that sits in front of the face, so the swimmer can keep their head still and focus on technique.
  4. Paddles and fins: paddles go on the hands to build strength and feel for the water; short training fins go on the feet to build kick speed and ankle flexibility.
  5. Bands, foam roller and skipping rope: for land training. These support strength and conditioning (S&C: training on land to support swimming), warm-ups and recovery.

What NOT to buy

Save your money. These won't help in lessons or training:

  • Beach goggles or swim masks. reason to give parents, e.g. fit or field of view
  • Yellow foam kickboards. reason, and whether the club provides kickboards or recommends a particular type
  • Equipment before your swimmer needs it. Wait until your swimmer's stage or squad uses it, and if you're unsure, ask the coach first.
  • any other items to avoid, e.g. armbands or nose clips

What this means for your swimmer

  • Start with a costume, a hat and pool goggles. That's all a beginner needs.
  • Add equipment as your swimmer moves up. Their coach or teacher will tell you when.
  • If you're not sure, ask before you buy. We'd rather you spent nothing than bought the wrong thing.
