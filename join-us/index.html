Join us

Tell us who's swimming and we'll point you to the right place, and explain exactly what happens next.

A quick, honest note on places. Leander is popular and places are limited, so waiting lists are common. A typical wait is typical wait for an Academy place and for a squad trial. We'd rather tell you that now than surprise you later.

Who's swimming?

A child aged 5–10 who is learning to swim

Start in the Academy, our learn-to-swim programme.

The Academy takes children from their first lesson to swimming all four strokes properly, in groups of up to 10 at Ernest Bevin, Tooting. Plenty of families join simply so their child can swim safely and well.

What happens next

  1. Join the Academy waiting list using our online form.
  2. When a place may be coming up, we'll contact you to arrange a short trial so we can see which stage suits your child. Trials run throughout the year. confirm how families are contacted and how the trial works
  3. If there's a place, your child starts in the right stage, and you pay the £48.75 annual membership plus the termly fee.

A child who already swims competitively, or in a school squad

Book a squad trial.

A squad is a competitive training group, grouped by ability and age. Ours run from Sparks to Mercury. If your child already trains with another club or a school squad, a trial helps our coaches find the squad that fits.

What happens next

  1. Fill in the squad trial form.
  2. We'll contact you to arrange a trial with a coach. what the trial involves, how long it takes and when trials run
  3. After the trial, the coach will recommend a squad. If there's no space yet, we'll tell you honestly and add you to the waiting list.

Before the trial, it helps to look at our squad standards. These show the typical age, the races a swimmer should be able to swim and the sessions each squad trains.

A teenager who wants to keep swimming without racing, or is easing off for exams

Diamond could be the right fit.

Diamond is our non-competitive squad and one of our biggest, with 38 swimmers. It's for teenagers who love swimming and want to stay fit and part of the team without the pressure of racing. It's also there for swimmers who need to ease off for GCSEs or A levels. Choosing Diamond is a positive choice, and swimmers can move back to a competitive squad later.

Diamond swimmers train at least once a week, and we recommend three sessions.

What happens next

  1. Email us to ask about a place. Tell us a little about your teenager's swimming so far.
  2. A coach will get back to you about sessions and availability. confirm Diamond is open to new joiners, not only swimmers moving from other squads
  3. Already in one of our competitive squads? Talk to your coach about moving to Diamond, and back again after exams.

An adult

Swim with Leander Masters.

Masters is for adults aged 18 and over: fitness swimmers, triathletes and people coming back to the water after time away. There are two sessions a week with qualified coaches, including Masters and triathlon specialists.

What happens next

  1. Email us to say you'd like to try a session, with a little about your swimming.
  2. We'll reply with session times and whether there's space. Masters session times, venue and fees

A swimmer with a disability

Talk to us. We're inclusive by design.

Swimmers with a disability train within our squads, alongside everyone else. Several of our coaches are qualified to coach disability swimming, including Head Coach Edgar Andrade, Caroline Findlay and Gregory Kat.

What happens next

  1. Get in touch and tell us about the swimmer: their age, their swimming so far and any support they need.
  2. We'll arrange a conversation with a coach to talk through the right squad and the support available. named contact for inclusion enquiries
Talk to us

Inclusive by design

Not sure which fits?

Email admin@leanderswim.com and tell us a little about the swimmer. We'll help you find where to start.

Want to help instead? Volunteer

Leander is not-for-profit and volunteer-run, so volunteers keep the club going. Officials are especially important: they're the trained volunteers (timekeepers, judges, referees) without whom galas can't run, and times can't count.

Anyone aged 15 or over can start with an online Judge 1 course, then train with a mentor on poolside, and qualify within a year. It's also a rewarding way to be at meets.
