Club

Volunteer and become an official

Leander is run by volunteers. Whether you can give an afternoon a year or a morning a month, your help keeps the club going and makes your swimmer's races count.

Sign up to helpThe officials pathway

Why we need you

Officials are trained volunteers, such as timekeepers, judges and referees. Without enough of them, a meet (a swimming competition) can't be licensed by Swim England, and the times swum there don't count for rankings or qualifying.

So when you volunteer as an official, you're not just helping out. You're making sure every swimmer's hard work is recognised. It's also a rewarding way to be at the meets your swimmer races in.

Ways to help

One-off help

Perfect if you're new, busy, or just want to try it.

  • Our Academy galas: helping at the galas where our youngest swimmers race
  • Leander galas 2027: we're hosting our own meets in 2027 and will need help on the day gala dates and roles needed
  • Cake sales at Ernest Bevin
  • The summer social
  • The Annual Squad Relay

Ongoing help

  • Become an official (see the pathway below)
  • Join a pillar group or the committee: help lead Performance, Participation, Community or administration. How the club works
  • other ongoing roles, e.g. team manager at away galas

The officials pathway, step by step

You don't need to know anything about swimming rules to start. You'll learn as you go, alongside experienced officials.

StepWhat happensHow long
1. Sign upTell us you're interested. We'll register you and explain the next steps.how to register, e.g. Swim England membership needed
2. Online Judge 1 courseComplete Swim England's online Judge 1 course. Open to anyone aged 15 or over, so teenage swimmers can do it too.course length
3. Mentored traineeWork at galas as a trainee, with an experienced official mentoring you.Over several galas
4. Qualified Judge 1Once signed off, you're a qualified Judge 1.Usually within a year of starting
5. Go furtherExperienced officials can progress to higher qualifications, such as referee.next qualifications and support available

What's a Judge 1? It's the first Swim England judging qualification. A Judge 1 watches swimmers to check that strokes, turns and finishes follow the rules.

What's a timekeeper? A timekeeper stands at the end of a lane and times each swimmer with a stopwatch. It's often where new volunteers start. confirm whether timekeeping needs its own course at the club

What to wear and bring

Officials follow a poolside dress code, and there's a short kit list.

  • Dress code: poolside dress code for officials
  • Kit list: officials' kit list

Pool halls are warm, so light layers help. And bring a water bottle.
