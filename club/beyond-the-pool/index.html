Beyond the pool

When you choose a club, you're not only choosing where your child swims. You're choosing what they can grow into. At Leander, swimming leads to jobs, qualifications, leadership and university.

Book a squad trialBecome an official

Teach: paid work in our Academy

Our Academy teaches more than 300 children aged 5–10 to swim. Every one of our teachers has a competitive swimming background.

Squad swimmers can train as teachers and work as paid Academy teachers. It's a real job: you're paid, you build experience for your CV, and you help the next generation learn to swim properly.

Officiate: learn how racing really works

An official is a trained volunteer, such as a timekeeper, judge or referee. Without officials, a meet can't be licensed and swimmers' times don't count.

  • Start at our Academy galas. Squad swimmers can get poolside experience helping at our own Academy galas, where our youngest swimmers race.
  • Become a Judge 1 from age 15. Judge 1 is the first Swim England judging qualification. The route is: an online course, then a mentored trainee period at galas, then qualification, usually within a year.

It's a skill that travels. Officials are needed at every level of the sport, and it's a great way to stay involved if you stop racing.

How to become an official

Duke of Edinburgh's Award (DofE)

The Duke of Edinburgh's Award (DofE) is a youth award that young people often do through their school. Volunteering at Leander, such as helping at galas or assisting with teaching, can count towards the volunteering section of your award.

How it works

  • You arrange it yourself. The club doesn't run DofE. Students organise their own volunteering by approaching the club and asking.
  • Who to talk to: who now arranges DofE volunteering. In the past this was arranged through the Academy volunteer lead, or by speaking to Caroline Findlay.
  • Which level counts: your DofE leader (often at your school) confirms whether your volunteering counts and at which level (Bronze, Silver or Gold). The club doesn't decide this.
  • Hours available: typical volunteer hours per week or per term

Lead: role models for younger swimmers

Leadership is part of what it means to be in our top squad. Mercury swimmers (our regional and national-level squad) lead warm-ups and act as role models for younger swimmers. It's written into our squad standards, because we think how you behave matters as much as how fast you swim.

At the Annual Squad Relay, our youngest squad swimmers race alongside our seniors, side by side with the swimmers they look up to.

Athlete education: learning from the best

Our Platinum and Mercury swimmers (our two most senior competitive squads) take part in regular clinics with Olympians, alongside education on the parts of athlete life that don't happen in the water:

  • Balancing school and training: how to manage early mornings, exams and racing.
  • Being a female athlete: nutrition, periods, and training and competing around the menstrual cycle.

Our next clinic is with Freya Anderson (Team GB, Tokyo and Paris Olympics) on 9 January 2027, with an education session from Dr Emily Jevons.

University and scholarships

Our squad swimmers regularly win places at the best swimming universities. In 2025/26, nine of our Mercury swimmers left for university.

There's no central list of university swimming programmes, so we show you how to research them and approach coaches directly. We also explain how UK university performance programmes and US college scholarships work, and how World Aquatics points (formerly FINA points) are used. These are scores that compare swims across events, ages and genders, so universities and US colleges can compare swimmers fairly.
